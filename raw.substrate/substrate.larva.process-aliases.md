## Atlas commentary

Do NOT copy this pattern — read it as **months of lived shorthand practice**, which is
exactly what the claviature session will need as grounding. Three design signals inside:

1. **The family-prefix grammar:** `cmd` (commands) · `rn` (regimes/skills — "run") ·
   `scm` (schemes/seats) · `trt` (traits) · `proc` (pipelines). Two-level invocation —
   `rn krpht`, `scm traj` — a namespace-then-mnemonic shape. The temple's current keyboard
   layer is flat aliases (`g-vega`, `tree-snapshot`); if the claviature goes hierarchical,
   this is the precedent for what felt natural to type.
2. **Where the weight accumulated:** `rn` has 14 entries — nearly half the registry.
   Behaviors (regimes, skills) earned more shorthand than tools or seats. Signal: the
   operator reaches for *mode switches* more often than *dispatch verbs*. A claviature
   should make mode-switching the cheapest keystroke.
3. **Role-aliases over seat-aliases:** `scm` intentionally double-maps — `thnk` → capcom,
   `wrtr` → scribe, `orch` → 42. The operator wanted to summon *a function* ("a thinker")
   without remembering which seat currently held it. The temple's roster fact-kind
   taxonomy (join by file-stem, display names move) solved the same instability from the
   other end. A claviature could expose role-verbs that resolve through the roster.

Also note the failure mode preserved here: `root.path` points at
`/home/hruzam/www/larva_dev/dev/` — a path that had already drifted from the real repo
location by the time of burial. Registries rot at the root first. The "derive, don't
register" lean exists because of graves like this one.
