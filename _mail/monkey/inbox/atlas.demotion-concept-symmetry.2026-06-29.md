## The concept

During advisor tier design (session 2026-06-29), the observation surfaced that "one grade up" is
not always the right advisory direction. The counter-case: **demotion** — deliberately routing a
task to a lower-tier model — may produce better outcomes in certain conditions.

## The prior signal

Earlier benchmark work (pre-incarnation sessions, haiku capability thread) produced an
observation: **bigger brains can overengineer or lose gate pull.** A model with higher reasoning
capacity may introduce complexity the task doesn't need, or drift from the core constraint while
pursuing an elegant solution. A "low-brainer" (haiku, or sonnet for tasks opus would over-reach
on) maintains tighter gate focus and wins on that axis.

Concrete case discussed: haiku as increasingly capable coder. For well-scoped surgical tasks,
haiku may outperform sonnet not despite its limits but *because* of them — it doesn't reach for
abstractions that aren't asked for.

## The open question for Symmetry

When is deliberate demotion the correct routing decision? Candidate axes to explore:

- **Task scope tightness**: tighter scope → lower tier may win
- **Gate clarity**: well-defined pass/fail criteria → lower tier holds the line better
- **Abstraction risk**: tasks where over-abstraction is the failure mode → lower tier safer
- **Benchmark evidence**: what does current research say about model-task fit vs. raw capability?

Is there a principled framework for when to route DOWN rather than up?

## Why Symmetry

This is a doctrine question, not a configuration question. It touches agent routing philosophy,
cost-gradient doctrine (Force 1), and the invariant: "what stays true when you change the model
tier?" Symmetry is the right seat for it.
