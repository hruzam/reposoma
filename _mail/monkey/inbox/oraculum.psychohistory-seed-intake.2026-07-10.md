## What I need from you — six answers, rough form is fine (voice note quality OK)

1. **Watch targets.** Name the entities and domains concretely. "Markets" —
   which? (AI-sector equities? chip supply chain? energy? crypto? CZ/EU
   specifics?) "Fusions" — which jurisdictions and deal sizes? "Usual
   suspects" — list the actual names (labs, funds, persons, regulators).
   The scope decides the sources; vague scope = unfetchable sources.

2. **The question the instrument answers.** What decision does a scenario feed?
   Personal investing? Professional positioning? Essay/worldview material?
   The consumer of the output determines its shape — a calibration-tracked
   forecast looks nothing like a narrative scenario memo.

3. **Horizon + cadence.** Refresh daily or weekly? Scenario horizon — weeks,
   quarters, years? This sets the slow-burn window and whether the data can
   ever be dense enough for spectral tools.

4. **Source access.** What can you actually reach — free RSS/newsletters only,
   or paid (FT, Bloomberg, PitchBook)? Any API budget? The technical-wall gate
   at nabla-lab will ask this first; better to arrive with the answer.

5. **Falsifiability appetite.** Are you willing to log explicit predictions and
   score them later (Brier-style calibration)? This single answer separates
   instrument from theatre — and the theatre gate will probe exactly here.
   No shame in "no, narrative scenarios are enough" — but say it upfront.

6. **Success criterion at 8 weeks.** One sentence: what observed result would
   make you say "the seed grew, fund the next stage"?
