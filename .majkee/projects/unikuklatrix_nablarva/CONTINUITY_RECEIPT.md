**Inheritance should help the next reader act correctly and recover deeper knowledge
when needed. A continuity receipt records whether that happened.**

The receiving head records briefly:

- What task, phase and sources it arrived with.
- Which decisions and distinctions it could use immediately.
- What needed unpacking, why, and whether it read directly or used a crawler.
- Missing routes, stale assumptions, contradictions and operator rescue.
- Reading, helper and correction costs where observed; otherwise `unknown`.
- Repairs made, remaining uncertainty and where the evidence now lives.

For consequential inherited knowledge, make three answers obtainable:

> What may I safely inherit? What must I verify?
> What would make this inherited view wrong?

Keep natural homes for operative contracts, decisions with reasons, observed
receipts, unresolved questions/anomalies and explanatory history. A small entry
points to them. Expand around the task; preserve sources and competing evidence.

## How the knowledge travels through generations

1. **Use:** the receiving incarnation writes a receipt from actual experience,
   including partial or failed recovery.
2. **Learn:** compare receipts; retain useful corrections and their reasons.
3. **Promote deliberately:** if the method earns its cost, place it in one shared
   guide/program home, with a thin skill or local README route for consumers.
4. **Carry:** later RUNBOOKs reference that home; current STATUS points to the
   relevant receipt and unfinished obligations.
5. **Preserve before pruning:** every surviving lesson has evidence, a destination
   and an owner. Deferred work has a trigger. Verify that the destination is usable.

This creates a feedback path: **inherited understanding → actual use → receipt →
checked correction → better inheritance**. A saved file or matching hash alone
does not prove successful inheritance, permanent preservation or lower total cost.
