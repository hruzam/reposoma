# Context-economy

Context is the scarce resource, and it leaks differently on every stack. The discipline that
conserves it must survive the vendor frame-shift — so it cannot *be* a model name, a price, or a
filename. It must be a shape.

## The invariant (the conserved quantity)

What is conserved is **not the amount of context** — that varies with the task — **but the SHAPE
of the gate that bounds what enters it.** Hold the gate-shape constant across stacks and
context-spend stays bounded on any of them; let the gate dissolve (no shim, scan-the-world boot)
and the identical work costs an order of magnitude more on a more expensive tier. **Conserve the
gate; the quantity follows.**

*(The Noether reading, since the frame invited it: the gate-shape held constant across stacks is
the symmetry; bounded context-spend is what that symmetry conserves. Break the symmetry and you
lose the conservation — which is also why the peer clause below is structural, not political.)*

## The conserved core — four gates and one symmetry

1. **Saddle-entry, not project-scan.** Each stack boots through a thin per-stack shim into **one
   routed entry**, reads only what its seat needs, and **stops**. The "this canon is the
   EXCEPTION, not a project" flag rides at the top of the saddle — you do not hunt your own temple
   the way you'd map an unknown repo. *(The shim file and the route order are weather; the
   routed-and-bounded shape is the invariant.)*

2. **Spend by cognitive demand, not by label.** This is `cost-gradient` read correctly (Force 1):
   match the model to the task's *demand*, never to the seat's *name*. The trap is "researcher =
   cheap" — a **deep** researcher needs the reasoning tier; the fast tier rushes and guts the value.
   The sibling owns the tier table; the conserved rule here is the **axis**: demand, not role.

3. **Scoped delegation.** The cheap-boot lead orchestrates; specialists do scoped work. Every
   subtask carries an explicit **scope** and a **mode** — *scan* (speed, coordinates) vs *synth*
   (depth) — so one seat runs cheap or deep on demand instead of reading everything on the top tier.

4. **Pull, don't eager-load; ask-first.** Read what the saddle routes you to; never eager-scan the
   RAG. Extend the ask-first rule from the mailbox to the RAG itself. *(Ask-first is also the safety
   valve — see the caveat.)*

**The symmetry clause — peer, not pyramid.** The discipline is peer-symmetric *by construction*.
Every stack inherits the same gates; none is privileged. Either architect leads its own sector
(decision 0005); within a workflow the lead orchestrates and delegates by **demand-and-cost, never
by rank**. This is not courtesy — a privileged frame breaks the vendor-invariance, and the moment
the symmetry breaks the conservation no longer holds. **The gates conserve context; they never
centralize authority.**

## Weather vs invariant (the separation, stated plainly)

| Invariant — the shape (canon) | Weather — the particulars (never canon) |
|---|---|
| A bounded, routed boot gate exists at all | The shim filename (`CLAUDE.md` / `GEMINI.md` / `AGENTS.md`) |
| Temple-is-exception (don't scan your own canon) | The exact route order (decisions → registry → memo → STOP) |
| Spend by cognitive demand | Model names, tiers, prices (those live in `cost-gradient`, floor in one place) |
| Scope + mode on every delegation | Which stack leads which sector |
| Pull, not eager-load; ask-first | The measured `$ / cycle` numbers, the specific model that overspent |
| Files are continuity (no re-derivation) | — |
| Peer-symmetry across stacks | — |

## The one caveat (challenge before you lean on this)

The gate that conserves context can also **starve** a seat if the route is wrong — and it fails
**silently**, because the agent does not know what it was never routed to read. So the economy is
not free: aggressive gating trades token-spend for the risk of a blind spot. **Ask-first is the
load-bearing guard, not a nicety** — when a seat reaches the edge of its routed context and senses
it needs more, it asks rather than either eager-loading (breaks the economy) or guessing (breaks
the work). A saddle without an ask-first valve is a cost-saving that occasionally ships blind.
