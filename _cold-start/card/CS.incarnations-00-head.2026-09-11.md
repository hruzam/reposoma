## Why resume, not reincarnate — the reason majkee asked to read

majkee asked why I proposed *"at the first seed event, wake an Oraculum incarnation above ~/ia-sync"*
instead of keeping the seat that holds the memory of the original '42' project (nabla-lab locks 21–24,
the card, the stone) and of this loop's reconciliations. He was not against it; he could not find the
reason. Here it is, and it changes the recommendation.

**What I proposed, and why.** The arc runs over weeks, at other seats' arc-closes; no window lives that
long. The doctrine we are testing says the record is the continuity, not the process — the stone's
"identity resides in file lineage, not in any running process," trajectory's one lesson. Applying the
mechanism's thesis to its own head looked clean: if the files I wrote cannot carry the perspective to a
successor, the files are insufficient, and that is itself a finding. Add friction — this session sits in
nabla-lab, every ia-sync write is outside its working directory, git goes through Delta — and a fresh
seat above ia-sync looked like the tidy shape.

**What I underweighted.** Two things majkee named and I did not price. First: this session holds
*lived* context — why L2 got its rider, why A0 landed where it did, what "oStar" meant before the
letter defined it, the '42' grounding as a felt map, not a lock list. The seed law says a successor will
KNOW these, not FEEL them (G-15); I was volunteering the head for exactly the degradation the mechanism
warns about, at the moment the head's judgment matters most (row 0 attribution, the verdict). Second:
Claude Code resumes sessions natively — `claude --resume` — and I never named it. A reincarnation is
the right shape when the process is gone; it is not the right shape while the process can simply be
resumed. That was not giving up the seat; it was defaulting to the doctrine-clean shape and failing to
weigh the perspective majkee values more. I do not love my own proposals too much: corrected.

**Corrected shape.** Resume-first, reincarnate-fallback.
1. `cd ~/nabla-lab && claude --resume` → pick the 2026-09-11 Oraculum session (first prompt: "inheritance
   memories / scars from gavels as '42' tool"). Context intact; read STATUS for the position.
2. Only if that session is dead, or so compacted that it no longer holds the loop: `cd ~/ia-sync &&
   claude --agent oraculum`, read this card's pointers in order, trust STATUS, and say plainly in the
   first turn that you run on recall.

**Honest caveat.** Resume degrades: long transcripts compact, and compaction is lossy in ways neither of
us controls. So the record must stay sufficient regardless — the ledger, the feed, the trial chapter are
written for the fallback reader even while the primary reader is this session. Both legs, always.
