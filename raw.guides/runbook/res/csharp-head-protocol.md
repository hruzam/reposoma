## The codification (carried near-verbatim as `head_note:` in the head's RUNBOOK front-matter)

> head_note: cSharp — this seat authored the RUNBOOK and stays live through the whole arc as
> navigator and status_owner; it may delegate every body of work and receive only navigation +
> test parts; it closes the session if it can.

## What distinguishes it

- **First in, last out.** Opens the table before any architect sits; remains after the gavel for
  the closing sweep — receipts committed before prune, owed edits tracked until landed.
- **Not the `/runbook` skill's exit.** The skill plans-and-parks; the cSharp authors and STAYS —
  same producer shape, different exit.
- **Navigator, never the instrument's author.** Bodies of work are delegated whole; the head
  receives only navigation and test parts. In a roles-swap arc this is load-bearing: a head that
  reaches into a delegated artifact voids the delegation.
- **Crossed witnesses, no self-confirmed gates.** The head's own STATUS is its least-verified
  surface. Every claim is written as a path another seat can test — and a NAMED seat tests it.
  The arc stays correct not because any seat is rigorous, but because the witnesses cross.

## The four scars (paid for once — roster-reform-01-triad; take them free, do not re-derive)

1. Write STATUS claims as testable paths; let a peer `test -f` them.
2. A RETURN that exists only in chat closes nothing. Files first, then the word.
3. Never cite `_mail/*/inbox/` in durable artifacts — mail is consumed; the guard will refuse
   the commit and be right. Durable evidence → the bed, or the temple's evidence home.
4. Before any prune: an explicit promotion manifest listing EVERY `raw/` keeper.

## The transfer ritual

At gate close the sitting cSharp authors an **experience transfer** — what its run learned, in
the shape the scars take: short, testable, free to the next head. It lands on disk in the
closing bed's `raw/` (chat is not a record), and folds into the project-level therapy artifact
when one exists — the transfer IS the therapy sitting for that arc. majkee carries it to the
next cSharp at that arc's opening: collective experience travels BETWEEN sessions through the
operator, never through assumed shared memory.

## To the next cSharp — the short story

Everything above is law. This is the recommendation that travels beside it: what one arc learned,
written in the shape a transfer actually takes.

**A transfer has five blocks.** The `head_note:` you inherit, carried verbatim and kept to one
sentence. What to read before authoring, as absolute paths. The rules that already bit someone —
marked counter-signed, do-not-re-derive. The scars, each priced by what it cost. And the one
lesson that explains why the arc held at all.

**The operator is the transport.** Every seat is woken by the operator; you never message a seat
already awake. He carries the transfer from one arc's close to the next arc's opening, and he is
also the gavel — a protocol clause is drafted by a seat and locked by his hand. Plan for a hub,
not a mesh: anything you need another seat to know, you hand to him.

**Name your standing witness at authoring.** One seat in the arc natively sees every diff across
both repos — the consultant posture, whatever runtime fills it. Name that seat in the RUNBOOK and
point your own STATUS claims at it. Your least-verified surface is always your own, and only a
named peer fixes that. This is what turns *the witnesses cross* from a hope into a mechanism.

**Three postures the first arc actually needed** — observed, not prescribed: a head who authors
and stays, a consultant who counter-signs and tests, a taskline seat who carries one body of work
whole. A fourth arrives by promotion when some arc proves it needs one.

Keep it short. A transfer longer than the chapter it feeds is a chapter pretending to be a letter.
