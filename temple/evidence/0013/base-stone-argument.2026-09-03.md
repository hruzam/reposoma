# FINDING 1 — the precedence stone

## (a) LOCATION — argued, not picked: `temple/precedence.md`, NOT `raw.canon/`, NOT `doctrine.md`

Three candidates, two rejected on structural grounds:

- **`raw.canon/` — REJECT (circularity).** raw.canon is a *domain*: a bucket of promoted invariants
  (`canon.mail-protocol`, `canon.context-economy`, …), each terminal in its own scope, each carrying
  `expresses_as`. The precedence stone must *order* every domain — including raw.canon itself (a
  `canon.*` entry can collide with Sella or a flag). A law that orders raw.canon cannot be *filed
  inside* raw.canon without becoming peer to the things it ranks → no apex, circular. Oraculum's
  "canon from ashes" instinct is right about *tone* (short, receipts-backed, grows on need) but
  wrong about *tier*: the apex cannot sit inside one of its own domains.
- **`temple/doctrine.md` — REJECT (wrong genus).** doctrine.md declares itself in line 9: *"Read it
  as advice, not law."* It is the architect's mentor-text — portable skeleton, half-life caveats,
  adapt-don't-obey. The precedence stone is the exact opposite genus: the *one hard law* the temple
  holds, the thing that is NOT advice. Folding a binding precedence order into a document whose
  whole frame is "advice, re-verify, adapt" dilutes the one clause that must never be adapted.
- **`temple/precedence.md` — ADOPT.** The temple layer is the sovereign root (AGENTS.md: "READ
  FIRST … the cross-project sovereign source"). It already holds three governance genera side by
  side: `doctrine.md` (advice), `decisions/` (gaveled locks), `roster/system-map/file-tree`
  (weather). The precedence stone is a *fourth, distinct* genus: the **order among all law-bearing
  documents**. Domain-neutral by construction, it belongs at the domain-neutral root, beside
  doctrine — not inside a domain it ranks. Adoption recorded as decision 0013, so the stone has a
  durable named address AND a gaveled lock; every WINS clause cites the *named file*, never the
  decision number.

## (b) THE STONE — as drafted (landed, lightly tightened, as `temple/precedence.md`)

Five-rank order: (1) the operator's live gavel (0002 · Force 4) · (2) the stone + `temple/decisions/`
locks · (3) a project's own flag/PROJECT.yaml within its bounds · (4) a domain guide's WINS clause,
terminal within its domain · (5) the live/dated-source carve-out on volatile facts (Sella L8 — not a
rank inversion). Rules: no doc below rank 2 declares itself supreme over another DOMAIN; collisions
resolve by OBJECT then rank, never authorship/recency; the stone grows only on a real collision
receipt.

# FINDING 2 — Sella ↔ session-shape boundary

The split (accepted, gaveled with 0013): **Sella** governs PRIMITIVE-BUILDING — how an agent, skill,
hook, or relay is authored: stochastic-compiler laws, description budgets, smallest-native-primitive,
verify-before-promote. *"Is this primitive well-made?"* The **session-shape canon**
(`raw.guides/{runbook,status,PAD,bus,gloss}`) governs SESSION SHAPE — framing, position, exchange,
closure: one gate, one present position, the bus cycle, the promotion boundary. *"Is this session
well-run?"* A primitive is BUILT under Sella and RUN inside a canon-shaped session; where they meet,
Sella owns the skill's construction, the canon owns the session it produces. Neither wins — different
objects. Residue → the stone. Consequence applied: Sella's precedence clause narrowed explicitly to
sibling *builder* guides; boundary paragraph landed in Sella GUIDE §Boundary + runbook GUIDE header.
